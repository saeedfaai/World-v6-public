# World 8 / Z0-A — Commercial Licensing Policy

**Classification:** PUBLIC POLICY / NON-CONFIDENTIAL  
**Purpose:** define the boundary between public review/citation and commercial exploitation.

## 1. No implied commercial license

Publication on Zenodo, GitHub, Software Heritage, academic platforms, social networks, or any other public venue does not itself grant a commercial-use right.

Commercial rights are governed by the applicable file-specific license and, where required, a separate written commercial agreement.

## 2. Examples of commercial use requiring review/licensing

Subject to the governing file/license terms, commercial activity may include:

- deploying World 8-derived systems in a paid product or service;
- incorporating substantial protected materials into enterprise software, SaaS, hosted services, or commercial agent platforms;
- white-labeling or reselling implementations;
- using protected architecture/documentation as a paid consulting deliverable beyond what the public license permits;
- sublicensing protected materials;
- embedding protected materials in commercial training, productization, or proprietary customer deployments where the applicable license does not permit that use.

## 3. Commercial agreement structure

A negotiated commercial license should address, as applicable:

- exact licensed IP and version;
- field of use;
- territory;
- term and renewal;
- number/type of deployments or users;
- exclusivity or non-exclusivity;
- fees, royalties, minimums, milestones, or revenue share;
- source-code access, escrow, or no-access terms;
- support, maintenance, updates, and service levels;
- modification and derivative-work rights;
- sublicensing and affiliate rights;
- audit/reporting rights;
- confidentiality;
- security and data responsibilities;
- warranties/disclaimers and limitation of liability;
- indemnities where negotiated;
- termination and post-termination rights;
- ownership of improvements and jointly developed IP;
- treatment of third-party components;
- governing law and dispute resolution.

## 4. Background IP vs. project IP

A commercial engagement should distinguish:

- **Background IP:** pre-existing World 8 architecture, documentation, software, methods, know-how, brands, and other assets brought into the deal.
- **Foreground IP:** new work created specifically during the engagement.
- **Third-party IP:** components governed by separate licenses or ownership.

No transfer of ownership should be inferred merely from payment for services, access, evaluation, or deployment.

## 5. Evaluation and pilot access

Pilot/evaluation access should be time-limited, purpose-limited, non-transferable, and subject to confidentiality and security conditions where non-public material is involved. Production use should require a separate written authorization if not already covered.

## 6. Brand and endorsement

A license to use technical materials does not automatically grant trademark, logo, naming, endorsement, certification, or partnership rights.

## 7. Patent rights

Copyright/documentation licensing does not automatically grant patent rights. Any patent license, if needed, should be stated expressly in the signed commercial agreement.

## 8. Contact/approval principle

Commercial permission should be documented in a signed agreement by the applicable rights holder or authorized licensing entity. Informal messages, public availability, repository access, or technical assistance should not be treated as a substitute for a signed commercial license.

## 9. Legal review

This policy is a public licensing framework, not a complete contract. Final commercial agreements should be reviewed for the jurisdictions, tax rules, sanctions/export rules, employment/contractor law, consumer law, privacy law, and sector-specific requirements relevant to the transaction.
