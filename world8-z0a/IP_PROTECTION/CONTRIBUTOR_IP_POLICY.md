# World 8 / Z0-A — Contributor IP Policy

**Classification:** PUBLIC POLICY / NON-CONFIDENTIAL

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes the minimum IP and provenance expectations for anyone contributing code, documentation, architecture, research material, datasets, tests, designs, prompts, schemas, workflows, or other materials to World 8.

It is not itself an employment agreement, contractor agreement, copyright assignment, patent assignment, or contributor license agreement. Where ownership must be transferred or licensed, a signed agreement should be used.

## 2. Contribution authority

A contributor should submit only material that the contributor has the legal right to contribute. Material copied from an employer, client, university, collaborator, proprietary dataset, confidential source, or third party must not be submitted unless the contributor has clear authority and all required licenses/permissions.

## 3. Provenance requirement

Every material contribution should be traceable to its contributor and date through repository history, signed agreement, task record, or equivalent evidence. Significant externally sourced material should retain source and license attribution.

## 4. Background IP

Contributors must identify relevant pre-existing intellectual property they are bringing into a project engagement when that IP is not intended to transfer. A signed agreement should define whether such Background IP is excluded, licensed, or assigned.

## 5. Newly created work

For employees, contractors, consultants, research collaborators, vendors, or other paid contributors, ownership of newly created World 8-specific work should be addressed expressly in a signed agreement before substantive confidential work begins.

The agreement should address, where legally available and appropriate:

- copyright ownership/assignment;
- patent/invention assignment and cooperation obligations;
- rights in source code, documentation, designs, schemas, datasets, tests, and improvements;
- confidentiality and trade-secret obligations;
- moral rights waivers/consents where lawful and needed;
- disclosure of third-party dependencies;
- return/deletion of confidential materials at termination.

## 6. No implied ownership transfer

Merging a pull request, accepting a document, paying an invoice, reviewing a design, or allowing repository access should not be relied on as proof that ownership transferred. The signed agreement should control.

## 7. Patent-sensitive contributions

If a contributor believes a contribution contains a new patentable mechanism that has not yet been publicly disclosed, the contributor should not publish it to the public repository before a patent strategy review. Use a private channel and private repository instead.

## 8. Confidential contributions

Confidential or trade-secret material must not be submitted to this public repository. Access-controlled private systems should be used and contributor access should follow least-privilege principles.

## 9. Third-party and open-source dependencies

Before introducing a dependency, dataset, model, sample, code fragment, or copied text, record its source and governing terms. Avoid introducing terms that conflict with the intended commercialization or distribution model without deliberate approval.

## 10. AI-assisted contributions

AI-assisted drafting or coding should not remove human responsibility for provenance, license compliance, confidentiality, security, correctness, or originality claims. Contributors must not place confidential third-party or project material into external AI services unless authorized.

## 11. Contributor identity record

For material contributions, maintain an internal record containing at least: legal/working name, contact, engagement type, agreement reference, contribution period, repository identity, and IP/confidentiality status. Do not publish personal or sensitive data unnecessarily.

## 12. Disputes and uncertainty

If ownership, authority, or licensing is unclear, pause publication/merger of the affected material and resolve the issue before relying on it commercially.
