# World 8 / Z0-A — Trade Secret Boundary

**Classification:** PUBLIC POLICY / NON-CONFIDENTIAL

## 1. Principle

World 8 should not depend on secrecy for material that is already published. Instead, secrecy should be reserved for future information whose economic value depends on remaining confidential and for which reasonable secrecy controls are actually maintained.

## 2. Public layer

The public layer may include:

- architecture principles and public specifications;
- published manuscripts and diagrams;
- DOI/SWHID/provenance evidence;
- intentionally published interface contracts;
- public claim boundaries and falsifiers;
- public reference implementations or examples that have been intentionally released.

## 3. Private moat layer

Unless deliberately approved for publication, keep private:

- production implementation details beyond the public baseline;
- operational recipes, tuning parameters, orchestration heuristics, routing policies, scoring logic, and optimization methods;
- deployment playbooks, reliability tricks, recovery procedures, and monitoring methods that create a practical advantage;
- customer-specific integrations, data mappings, workflow logic, and business rules;
- sales/negotiation playbooks, lead-scoring logic, pricing strategy, unit economics, supplier/customer terms, and commercial forecasts;
- private datasets, enrichment methods, internal benchmarks, and evaluation corpora;
- security controls, credentials, secrets, vulnerability details, incident data, and internal access architecture;
- unpublished invention improvements and patent drafting materials;
- private model-selection, provider-arbitrage, cost-control, caching, or execution strategies that create commercial advantage.

## 4. Reasonable secrecy measures

For information intended to be treated as a trade secret, apply practical controls such as:

- access-controlled private repositories/storage;
- role-based and least-privilege access;
- individual accounts and audit logs where available;
- NDA/confidentiality provisions before disclosure;
- employee/contractor IP and confidentiality agreements;
- clear confidential markings on sensitive documents;
- segmented access so one person does not receive unrelated secrets;
- secure credential management and regular rotation;
- access review and prompt offboarding;
- restrictions on copying sensitive data into external AI, cloud, chat, or collaboration systems unless authorized;
- periodic inventory of confidential assets and who has access.

## 5. Disclosure decision rule

Before moving an item from private to public, determine whether publication is strategically necessary. Once a trade secret is intentionally and lawfully published, trade-secret protection for that disclosed information may be lost.

If the item might be patentable, obtain patent-strategy review before disclosure. If the item derives value from secrecy but does not need publication, keep it private.

## 6. Partner disclosure

When sharing private material with investors, customers, suppliers, universities, contractors, or strategic partners, disclose only what is necessary. Prefer staged disclosure:

1. public architecture/overview;
2. NDA and purpose limitation;
3. controlled technical disclosure;
4. deeper access only after commercial/technical diligence justifies it.

## 7. Evidence without disclosure

A confidential asset can still be evidenced without publishing its substance. Internally retain timestamps, signed contributor/inventor records, file hashes, private Git commits, access logs, and notarized/escrowed evidence where legally useful. A public register may reference only a non-revealing asset ID.

## 8. No retroactive secrecy claim

This policy does not claim that already-public World 8 material is secret. It defines the boundary for future protected know-how and confidential information.
